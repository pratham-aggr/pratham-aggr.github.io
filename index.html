fast, physically faithful neural climate surrogates

My research asks one question: can inexpensive, targeted interventions make neural climate surrogates both fast and physically faithful? At the Climate Analytics Lab within Scripps Institution of Oceanography, under Duncan Watson-Parris, I built FASST — a compact atmosphere emulator that runs ~18× faster than larger autoregressive models and stays stable over a 140-year ocean-coupled simulation. Fast surrogates like these tend to lose physical variability, such as ENSO, when trained on MSE alone; I study low-cost fixes — stochastic fine-tuning and probing & steering a model's internal representations — drawing on my work steering language models at inference time in the Belkin Lab. My long-term goal is to build efficient, trustworthy ML models for atmospheric and ocean science.

2025–present Climate Analytics Lab, Scripps ML Researcher

FASST — a compact atmosphere emulator for ocean coupling

Built FASST, a compact, non-autoregressive atmosphere emulator (7.7M parameters) coupled to the physics-based POP2 ocean. Its atmospheric inference runs ~18× faster than CAMulator, a 751M-parameter autoregressive emulator, and the coupled model stays stable over a 140-year simulation without SST restoring. Trained on MSE alone it reproduces damped ENSO variability and a cold bias in the tropical Pacific — the gap between strong regression skill and physical fidelity that now drives my work on inexpensive interventions to recover lost variability.

ClimateBench 2.0 — benchmarking climate models fairly

With Willa Tobin, under Duncan Watson-Parris, contributed to ClimateBench 2.0, an architecture-independent framework for evaluating physics-based and machine-learning climate models on a common set of predictive-skill metrics. Benchmarked 14 CMIP6 models across MAE, RMSE, and CRPS — a hands-on lesson that no single headline number captures skill, and that a model topping one metric can fail on another. Built efficient, at-scale preprocessing for NetCDF, Zarr, GRIB, and GeoTIFF data streamed from Google Cloud; work recognized with a $4,500 research scholarship.

Inference-time steering — a transferable fix for surrogates

A surrogate with high R² can still get the physics wrong, and retraining it for every flaw is costly and often counterproductive. With Dr. Qingsong Wang, I extracted topic-specific refusal directions from LLaDA-8B's internal activations to steer its behavior at inference time, without retraining; in my capstone (DSC 180A/B) with Professor Weng I extended this to make refusals explainable. I now want to carry this idea to climate: probing and steering FASST's internal representations to recover ENSO variability lost during training, while preserving long-term coupled stability.
